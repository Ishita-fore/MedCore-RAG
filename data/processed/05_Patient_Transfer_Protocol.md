**Purpose and scope**

This protocol provides an operational reference for internal and approved external patient transfers. It focuses on identity, destination, ownership, handoff, transport, arrival reconciliation and exception handling. Clinical suitability and clinical handoff remain the responsibility of authorized clinical teams.

**1. Transfer initiation**

A transfer begins when an authorized clinical decision or approved pathway requires movement to another location. The operational record should identify the sending location, intended destination and accountable receiving function. The request should remain visible while prerequisites are being completed.

**Key actions**

• Verify patient and encounter.

• Identify sending and receiving functions.

• Confirm intended destination.

• Identify prerequisites.

• Assign an owner.

**Operational record and handoff**

The record should show the current status, destination and next action so the receiving team does not need to reconstruct the request.

**Exception and escalation**

If the transfer cannot proceed, keep the request open and escalate the dependency through the approved route.

**2. Identity and records**

The correct patient and encounter must be established before transfer information is attached. Identity discrepancies should be handled through the authorized registration or records process. Operational staff should not improvise merges, deletions or corrections.

**Key actions**

• Use approved identifiers.

• Stop when records conflict.

• Route discrepancies to the authorized function.

• Preserve traceability.

• Confirm resolution before proceeding.

**Operational record and handoff**

The transfer record should remain associated with the authoritative encounter.

**Exception and escalation**

An identity conflict is a stop condition for the affected transfer activity until the authorized process resolves it.

**3. Destination and capacity**

Destination coordination connects the request with actual capacity. Availability can change, so a proposed bed or unit should not be treated as confirmed until the approved workflow establishes the current state.

**Key actions**

• Confirm destination.

• Check current capacity.

• Confirm receiving ownership.

• Record changes.

• Keep capacity delays visible.

**Operational record and handoff**

The operational record should show whether the destination is proposed, confirmed, unavailable or changed according to the approved workflow.

**Exception and escalation**

If capacity changes, update the status and communicate the new plan rather than relying on an old message.

**4. Clinical handoff and operational handoff**

Clinical teams conduct the clinical handoff under their professional procedures. Operations preserves the logistical information needed to complete the transition and identifies pending operational work.

**Key actions**

• Identify sender and receiver.

• Confirm current location.

• List operational pending actions.

• Capture acknowledgement where required.

• Use approved communication channels.

**Operational record and handoff**

A concise operational handoff should tell the receiving function what has been completed and what remains open.

**Exception and escalation**

If acknowledgement is not received, keep the transition open and use the approved escalation route.

**5. Transport and movement**

Transport is coordinated through the approved hospital process. Clinical staff determine clinical transport requirements, while Operations coordinates timing, destination and support arrangements within its role.

**Key actions**

• Confirm approved transport.

• Confirm destination and timing.

• Coordinate assigned support.

• Record material delays.

• Reconcile arrival.

**Operational record and handoff**

Transport status should remain linked to the transfer workflow so that a delayed arrangement is visible to the accountable owner.

**Exception and escalation**

A missed or unsuitable arrangement should remain open and be escalated through the designated route.

**6. Internal transfers**

Internal movement may occur between wards, the ED, ICU or other approved locations. The common operational controls are identity, destination, ownership, handoff and arrival reconciliation.

**Key actions**

• Verify patient and encounter.

• Confirm receiving location.

• Coordinate handoff.

• Update location.

• Reconcile the receiving state.

**Operational record and handoff**

The record should follow the transition from the sending location to the actual receiving location.

**Exception and escalation**

If the patient arrives somewhere other than planned, reconcile the actual location before closure.

**7. External transfers**

External transfers require an approved receiving pathway and appropriate documentation. Operations coordinates acceptance, destination and transport within its assigned role.

**Key actions**

• Confirm receiving organization.

• Confirm acceptance where required.

• Coordinate approved transport.

• Provide authorized documentation.

• Keep open dependencies visible.

**Operational record and handoff**

External acceptance should be visible before the transfer is treated as complete.

**Exception and escalation**

If acceptance is not confirmed, the transfer remains open and the responsible function should be identified.

**8. Delays and downtime**

Delays can arise from capacity, transport, documentation, receiving acceptance or system issues. The operational response should keep the reason at the level known, owner and next action visible.

**Key actions**

• Record the dependency.

• Assign an owner.

• Record the next action.

• Use approved downtime procedures.

• Reconcile temporary records after restoration.

**Operational record and handoff**

Open exceptions should remain visible during shift changes and should not depend on informal memory.

**Exception and escalation**

Do not create an unofficial permanent workflow during downtime; use the approved procedure and reconciliation route.

**9. Communication and privacy**

Transfer communication should be factual, necessary and directed to an appropriate recipient. Clinical questions should be routed to authorized clinical personnel.

**Key actions**

• Use approved channels.

• Confirm recipient.

• Share only necessary information.

• Record material operational communication.

• Protect confidential information.

**Operational record and handoff**

Documentation should be sufficient for continuity without unnecessary duplication of sensitive information.

**Exception and escalation**

When communication scope is uncertain, use the approved supervisory or escalation route.

**10. Completion and review**

A transfer is complete only when the approved receiving state has been established and relevant operational records are reconciled. Review can then identify recurring coordination issues.

**Key actions**

• Confirm actual destination.

• Reconcile bed/resource status.

• Confirm receiving ownership.

• Close or formally hand over open work.

• Record improvement actions where required.

**Operational record and handoff**

The final record should make it clear that the transition has reached the approved operational state.

**Exception and escalation**

Recurring issues should be routed to the relevant process owner rather than repeatedly recorded without follow-up.
