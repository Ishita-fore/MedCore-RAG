## MedCore Hospitals &amp; Healthcare Services Pvt. Ltd.

## Employee Handbook

## 1. About This Handbook

The Employee Handbook is the common reference for employees working at MedCore Hospitals. It describes the organization-wide expectations that apply across clinical, administrative and support functions while leaving detailed operating procedures to specialist HR and departmental policies. Employees should use this handbook to understand the overall employee lifecycle: joining the organization, establishing attendance, taking leave, receiving compensation and benefits, working with colleagues, protecting information, developing skills, participating in performance reviews, raising concerns and completing separation. The handbook is not intended to replace an appointment letter. Individual employment terms, including the employee's position, compensation and contractual notice provisions, are controlled by the employee's appointment or engagement documentation. Employees are expected to read the handbook and policies relevant to their role. Managers are expected to communicate role-specific requirements and escalate questions that

cannot be resolved through the normal process.

## 2. Employment Categories and Status

MedCore uses different employment arrangements to support hospital operations and corporate functions. For internal administration, employees may be recorded as permanent, probationary, fixed-term, trainee/intern or approved contractual personnel. The employment category affects the applicable onboarding documentation, probation or contract review, benefit eligibility, notice arrangements and some HR workflows. It does not remove the requirement to comply with workplace conduct, confidentiality, information security and safety requirements. Permanent employees occupy ongoing roles subject to confirmation and the terms of their appointment. Probationary employees are assessed during an initial period. Fixed-term employees have a defined contractual end date. Trainees and interns work under structured learning or project arrangements, while contractual personnel follow the controls in their approved engagement. HR maintains the employee's category in the HR master record. If an employee believes the recorded category is incorrect, the matter should be raised with HR

rather than changed informally.

## 3. Joining and Employee Records

Joining is completed after HR verifies the documents and approvals required for the employee's category. Employees are responsible for providing accurate information and for reporting material changes promptly. Typical joining records may include appointment documentation, identity and address evidence where required, education or experience evidence relevant to the role, payroll or bank information, emergency contacts and policy acknowledgements. HR records may contain sensitive information such as contact details, compensation-related

information, emergency contacts and employment history. Access to these records is role-based

and employees should access another person's record only when their role requires it. A discrepancy between a submitted document and the HR record should be reported through the approved HR process.

Employees must not create parallel unofficial records or submit altered, misleading or knowingly incomplete documents. Changes to bank details or other sensitive employee information should be completed through the approved HR workflow so that the change can be verified.

## 4. Probation, Confirmation and Internal Movement

Probation allows the employee and manager to assess role fit, work quality, reliability, conduct and ability to meet responsibilities. Feedback should be provided during probation rather than being concentrated at the end. For the synthetic MedCore framework, the standard probation period is six months from the joining date.

At the end of the period, the manager may recommend confirmation, extension or separation based on documented performance and role requirements. HR reviews and records the final decision. An extension should identify the specific areas requiring improvement, the expected standard and the review point.

Employees should receive a clear communication of the extended status rather than relying on an informal conversation. Transfers, promotions, reporting-manager changes and department movements are processed through HR after the required business approvals. The employee should not treat a proposed move as effective until the approved change has been communicated.

## 5. Working Hours, Rosters and Attendance

Attendance records support workforce planning, payroll and leave administration. MedCore operates both clinical and non-clinical functions, so approved rosters may differ by department. For the synthetic framework, the standard administrative schedule is 9:30 a.m. to 6:00 p.m., Monday to Friday, including a one-hour break.

Clinical and operational teams may work approved shifts instead. Employees are responsible for recording their own attendance through the designated system. A missed punch, incorrect entry or roster discrepancy should be reported promptly through the attendance-correction process.

Employees must not record attendance for another person. Working remotely, changing a shift, arriving late or leaving early requires the approval applicable to the employee's role. Repeated unexplained attendance discrepancies may be escalated by the reporting manager to HR.

A request to correct attendance does not itself mean that the record has been corrected.

## 6. Leave and Absence

Leave is approved time away from work. The separate Leave &amp; Attendance Policy contains detailed leave categories, entitlements, approvals, carry-forward rules and attendance treatment; this handbook establishes the employee's general responsibilities. Planned leave should be requested before the intended absence wherever practical.

An unexpected absence should be communicated to the reporting manager as soon as reasonably possible and followed by the required HR or attendance record. Submitting a leave request does not mean the leave is automatically approved. Employees should confirm approval status and arrange handover of important work before relying on the leave.

During an extended absence, the employee should maintain the communication required by the applicable policy. Where supporting evidence is required, it should be provided through the approved channel. Employees returning from leave should complete any required attendance correction or return-to-work documentation promptly.

## 7. Compensation and Payroll Administration

Payroll uses approved HR master data, compensation terms, attendance and leave inputs and other authorized changes. Employees should review payslips and raise discrepancies through HR or Finance with the relevant pay period and component identified. For the synthetic MedCore framework, monthly payroll is targeted for the last working day of the month, subject to bankprocessing constraints.

This is an invented dataset value and is not a real payroll commitment. Salary, employee reimbursement, travel advances and patient-account financial transactions are different processes. An employee business expense must be handled through the Employee Reimbursement Policy rather than being added informally to salary.

Employees should keep payroll and bank information current through the approved HR process and should not share another employee's payslip or payroll information. Where an amount is unclear, employees should seek clarification rather than altering records or attempting an informal adjustment.

## 8. Benefits and Employee Welfare

MedCore provides employee benefits subject to employment category, tenure, grade and the terms of the applicable benefit schedule. HR maintains the controlled benefit information and communicates changes. The separate Employee Benefits workbook is the detailed source for benefit categories, eligibility conditions and limits.

The handbook therefore describes the framework rather than reproducing every numerical benefit. Employees should confirm eligibility before submitting a benefit request. Receiving a benefit in another employee's case does not establish eligibility for every employee.

Health-related employee benefits are distinct from patient billing. An employee treatment concession, where applicable, is an HR benefit and should not be recorded as a discretionary patient discount or refund by Finance. Questions about benefit eligibility, enrollment or documentation should be directed to HR or the designated benefits administrator.

## 9. Professional Conduct

MedCore expects employees to behave professionally, respectfully and responsibly while working, representing the organization externally or using organizational communication channels and systems. Professional conduct includes respectful communication, responsible use of authority, truthful records, appropriate workplace boundaries and compliance with authorized procedures. A disagreement with a decision is not automatically misconduct; employees should use appropriate escalation channels.

Employees must not make unauthorized commitments on behalf of MedCore. This includes commitments relating to employment decisions, patient services, refunds, payments, contracts or confidential information. Managers should address conduct concerns consistently and consult HR for serious or repeated matters. Employees should avoid intimidation, humiliation, threats, deliberate obstruction or retaliation. Organizational records must not be falsified. This includes attendance, expense, employee, patient, procurement, approval and operational records.

## 10. Respect, Inclusion and Equal Opportunity

MedCore's employee framework is intended to support fair employment decisions based on role requirements, qualifications, performance, conduct and legitimate business needs. Employees should not be subjected to adverse treatment because of a personal characteristic unrelated to the requirements of their work. Concerns about exclusion, disrespect or inappropriate workplace behavior may be raised with a manager, HR or the designated alternate channel. Managers should not discourage a good-faith concern. Where a concern involves the reporting manager, the employee may approach HR or the alternate formal route instead of remaining within that reporting line. Employees participating in an authorized review are expected to provide truthful information and maintain appropriate confidentiality. HR handles formal concerns under the relevant grievance or misconduct process. Employees are

not expected to determine the final classification of a complaint before reporting it.

## 11. Harassment, Misconduct and Formal Complaints

Serious workplace concerns may include repeated unwanted conduct, threatening behavior, humiliating treatment, inappropriate messages, misuse of authority or other conduct requiring formal review. A report should provide useful facts such as dates, locations, people involved, what occurred and available evidence. The person reporting the concern does not need to determine its final policy or legal classification. HR determines the appropriate process, which may include an initial assessment, referral to a designated committee, management action or another approved route. Information about a complaint is shared only with people who need it for the authorized process. Employees should not independently investigate serious complaints, confront another person on behalf of the organization or delete relevant messages and records after becoming aware of a formal concern. Retaliation against a person who raises a good-faith concern or participates in an authorized

review is not an acceptable workplace response.

## 12. Health, Safety and Workplace Readiness

Employees must follow safety instructions appropriate to their work area. Clinical areas, laboratories, stores, kitchens, offices and technical spaces may have different safety controls. An employee who observes an immediate hazard should take a safe, reasonable action and notify the responsible supervisor or designated facilities or safety channel. Incidents and near misses should be reported so corrective actions can be tracked. Employees should follow access restrictions, safety signage and protective-equipment requirements. They must not disable alarms or other safety controls without authorization. Spills, electrical hazards, damaged equipment and unsafe conditions should be reported

promptly. Employees should not enter restricted clinical, technical or storage areas without authorization. Mandatory safety or emergency training relevant to the employee's role should be completed by the assigned deadline.

## 13. Emergency Response and Business Continuity

During an emergency, employees should prioritize immediate safety, follow site instructions and cooperate with designated response personnel. The appropriate response depends on the event and location. For fire or evacuation, employees follow the designated route and assembly instructions.

For a security event, employees follow security instructions. For a utility disruption, employees follow facilities direction. For an IT outage, employees should use the approved downtime procedure rather than creating uncontrolled parallel records that could conflict with the system of record after recovery.

Managers should account for team members where the emergency process requires it and communicate operational constraints to the responsible function. Employees should not improvise specialist emergency responses beyond their training. Incident records should be completed through the designated channel after immediate safety has been addressed.

## 14. Confidentiality and Information Handling

Employees may encounter confidential information about patients, employees, vendors, finances, operations, contracts and internal decisions. Access is granted for business need, not personal curiosity. Information should be used only for authorized work and shared through approved channels.

Printed documents, screenshots, exports and spreadsheets can create the same confidentiality risk as information viewed inside a system. Employees should verify recipients before sharing sensitive information and should not send confidential records to personal email or unauthorized storage. Workstations should be locked when unattended.

Printed confidential material should not be left in common areas. Lost devices, accidental disclosures and suspected unauthorized access should be reported promptly. The Data Privacy &amp; Confidentiality Policy and Information Security Policy provide more detailed controls.

This handbook establishes the common employee responsibility to protect information throughout its lifecycle.

## 15. Technology, Credentials and Acceptable Use

MedCore technology resources are provided to support business operations. Employees are expected to protect assigned accounts, devices and organizational information. Each employee is responsible for activity performed through their assigned account.

Passwords, authentication factors and access tokens must not be shared. Suspected compromise should be reported to IT. Employees should not install unauthorized software, connect unknown storage media to protected systems or bypass technical controls.

Approved collaboration and file-sharing tools should be used for organizational information. Limited personal use may be tolerated where it does not interfere with work, create security risk, violate policy or consume unreasonable resources. Unexpected requests for credentials, payments or confidential information should be verified through a trusted channel.

Phishing, malware and suspicious access attempts should be reported to IT or Information Security.

## 17. Conflicts of Interest, Gifts and Outside Activities

A conflict of interest may arise when a personal financial interest, family relationship, outside employment or other connection could influence or appear to influence a MedCore decision. A potential conflict does not automatically mean misconduct. Employees should disclose relevant circumstances to the manager or HR/Compliance channel so that the organization can determine an appropriate mitigation, reassignment or review.

Employees involved in procurement, vendor selection, recruitment, contracting or financial approval have a heightened responsibility to disclose relevant relationships. Employees should not accept a personal benefit linked to a business decision without checking the applicable rule. A gift received unexpectedly should be disclosed through the designated process where required.

Outside activities should be disclosed when they could affect working hours, confidentiality, performance or the employee's ability to act independently for MedCore.

## 19. Performance, Feedback and Improvement

Performance management is an ongoing process. Employees should understand role expectations, deliverables, quality standards and behavioral requirements. Managers should provide timely feedback and document significant discussions.

The separate Performance Management Policy contains the detailed goal-setting, review, rating and improvement framework. This handbook describes the employee's responsibilities within that framework. Employees should clarify unclear expectations early and raise constraints before deadlines where possible.

Feedback should be discussed constructively and agreed improvement actions should be documented when required. Managers should distinguish capability gaps, resource constraints, conduct concerns and isolated mistakes. Performance records should be factual, relevant to the role and handled confidentially.

Where performance concerns continue, HR may support a structured improvement process with defined expectations, review dates and documented outcomes.

## 20. Grievances, Speak-Up and Resolution Channels

Employees may raise workplace concerns through the reporting manager, HR or a designated alternate channel. HR is appropriate for employment conditions, workplace relationships, policy interpretation or matters requiring independent handling. When the reporting manager is directly involved in the concern, the employee may approach HR or the alternate formal route.

Serious allegations should be handled through the applicable formal procedure rather than informal workplace discussion. A useful concern record identifies the issue, relevant dates, people involved, available evidence and the outcome requested where appropriate. Employees should avoid circulating allegations while a matter is under review.

Employees may ask HR for clarification about which policy applies. A policy question should identify the document or situation clearly so that HR can refer to the controlled version. Good-faith reporting is protected within the organization's process, and retaliation concerns should themselves be escalated.

## 21. Disciplinary Review and Corrective Action

When an employee may have breached a policy or workplace requirement, MedCore may initiate a fact-finding review. The purpose is to establish what happened and determine an appropriate response based on documented information. Possible corrective responses may include coaching, a documented warning, an improvement plan, temporary restriction of a responsibility or access, reassignment or separation in accordance with the employee's terms and internal process.

The response depends on the circumstances. A one-time accidental error may require a different response from repeated non-compliance or serious misconduct. Employees are expected to cooperate with authorized reviews and provide truthful information.

Managers should consult HR before issuing formal disciplinary action. Disciplinary information is confidential and should be shared only with authorized personnel.

## 22. Separation, Notice and Handover

Employment may end through resignation, completion of a fixed-term arrangement, retirement, organizational decision or another authorized route. HR coordinates the exit process and confirms the relevant dates and documentation. For the synthetic MedCore framework, the standard resignation notice period for confirmed permanent employees is 60 calendar days unless the employee's appointment terms specify otherwise.

Probationary and fixed-term employees may have different terms. During notice, employees remain responsible for normal duties, confidentiality, information security, asset protection and appropriate handover. A practical handover should identify open work, deadlines, contacts, files, approvals, recurring activities and dependencies.

Managers should confirm which responsibilities are transferred and when. Employees should not remove organizational information when preparing to leave. Continuing confidentiality obligations are addressed in the relevant employment and separation documents.

## 23. Exit Clearance and Final Settlement

Exit clearance confirms that organizational assets, system access, financial items and HR records have been addressed. The employee returns assigned property and completes department-specific clearance activities. IT closes or transfers system access at the authorized time.

Administration confirms return of identification cards, laptops, equipment, keys and other assigned assets. The employee should not continue using organizational credentials after access is disabled. Finance checks outstanding advances, reimbursements and other employee financial items.

Employees should submit eligible final expense claims and settle advances through the approved process. HR completes final employee-record and exit documentation after the required clearances are addressed. If an employee believes an item is incorrect, the discrepancy should be raised promptly with the responsible function.

An exit is not complete merely because the employee's last working day has passed; the organization may still need to complete controlled financial, asset and access closure.

## 24. Employee Responsibilities After Separation

Confidentiality responsibilities do not end simply because employment ends. Former employees must not retain or misuse confidential MedCore information, credentials, internal records or protected data. Organizational property and access credentials must be returned or disabled as part of exit.

Files stored on personal devices or accounts should be handled according to the applicable organizational instruction. Where continuing obligations exist, they are governed by the appointment, confidentiality agreement or separation documentation. This handbook does not create additional post-employment restrictions beyond those separately authorized.

Former employees seeking employment letters, service records or other HR documents should use the designated HR channel. Employees should not represent themselves as current MedCore employees after the effective separation date.

## 25. Policy Governance, Exceptions and Document Control

The Employee Handbook is a controlled HR document owned by HR &amp; Administration. HR maintains the master version and communicates material revisions. Employees should use the current controlled copy.

If two controlled documents appear to conflict, employees should not choose the interpretation that is most convenient. The issue should be escalated to HR with the document names, versions and relevant sections. An exception requires authorization through the appropriate HR or management route.

An informal instruction from a colleague does not automatically amend a controlled policy. The handbook should be reviewed at least annually or after a material organizational or process change. Changes should be versioned so employees can identify the current edition.

Employee acknowledgement records that the employee has received access to the handbook; it does not replace the appointment letter or detailed policy-specific requirements.

## 26. Quick Reference and Scenario Guide

The handbook is designed to work as a navigation layer over the detailed HR and Finance documents. The following examples illustrate how an employee should identify the next controlled source rather than attempting to resolve every question from this handbook alone. An employee planning leave should use the Leave &amp; Attendance Policy for entitlement and approval details.

An employee approaching the end of probation should use the Recruitment &amp; Onboarding Policy for the detailed confirmation workflow. A performance concern should move to the Performance Management Policy. An employee asking about a business hotel reimbursement should use the Employee Reimbursement Policy.

A question about a patient discount or refund belongs to Finance &amp; Billing rather than the employee benefits process. A serious workplace complaint should move to the Complaint &amp; Grievance process. A question about confidential patient or employee information should move to the Data Privacy &amp; Confidentiality Policy and, where relevant, Information Security.

This cross-document structure is intentional for the enterprise RAG application: the handbook supplies broad context while detailed policies supply specific rules, limits and workflows.

## 27. Related Documents and Final Employee Checklist

Employees should consult the controlled policy that governs the specific activity. Key related documents include Leave and Attendance Policy, Recruitment and Onboarding Policy, Performance Management Policy, Employee Benefits Schedule, Complaint and Grievance Policy, Data Privacy and Confidentiality Policy, Information Security Policy and Employee Reimbursement Policy. Before acting on an HR-related question, an employee should identify the current document, confirm whether the rule applies to their employment category, check whether an approval is required and retain any evidence required by the process.

Before submitting a request, confirm the dates, employee identifier, department, supporting evidence and approval status. For sensitive matters, use the approved channel rather than personal email or informal messaging. Before leaving MedCore, complete handover, return assets, settle financial advances and claims, complete required HR forms and stop using organizational systems at the authorized time.
