## Document Control

Controlled internal guideline for patient-facing communication. Staff should use the current version and approved communication channels.

## 1. Purpose and Scope

These guidelines establish a consistent standard for patient-facing communication. They apply to reception, Patient Services, scheduling, billing-facing staff and other authorized employees. Communication should be clear, factual, respectful, appropriately limited and aligned with the employee's role.

| Control      | Standard practice                                           | Responsible role   | Escalation trigger                   |
|--------------|-------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------|--------------------------------------|
| Routine      | Follow the approved workflow and document material actions. | Process owner      | Exception cannot be resolved.        |
| Verification | Use authorized information and do not guess.                | Assigned staff     | Identity or source is uncertain.     |
| Traceability | Keep status, owner and next step visible.                   | Department manager | Case becomes overdue or conflicting. |

## 2. In-Person Communication

Staff should identify the request, determine whether verification is required and use an appropriate setting for sensitive discussion.

Patients should receive a clear next step. Staff should not argue, speculate or promise outcomes outside their authority.

## 3. Telephone Verification

## 6. Appointment Communication

Appointment messages should confirm date, time, location and approved preparation or attendance requirements, together with the cancellation or rescheduling route. Administrative staff should not add unapproved clinical instructions.

## 8. Clinical Boundaries

Administrative employees should not interpret diagnoses, reports, medication instructions or test results. Such requests should be routed to the relevant clinical team. Administrative staff may provide information that is clearly within their role.

## 9. Complaints

Complaint conversations should focus on understanding the concern, recording facts and explaining the next step. Staff should not state that an allegation is proven before review. Escalation should be used when the matter exceeds the employee's authority.

A patient asking for result interpretation should be routed to clinical staff. A billing dispute should be routed to Finance.
