**Document Control**

Controlled operational SOP for appointment management. Staff should use the current approved version and record material appointment changes through the designated system.

**1. Purpose and Scope**

This SOP establishes the standard process for appointment creation, verification, modification, cancellation, waitlist management and daily reconciliation. It applies to scheduling staff, reception, Patient Services and specialty coordinators. It supports accurate capacity use, correct patient association, clear communication and consistent escalation.

**2. Appointment Types**

Approved appointment types may include new consultation, follow-up, diagnostic service, planned procedure and tele-consult. Staff should identify the service, specialty, stated reason, expected duration and any administrative prerequisites before selecting a slot. Incorrect appointment types can create capacity and communication problems.

**3. Patient Identity Check**

The correct patient record should be located before an appointment is attached. Staff should use available identifiers and stop if identity remains uncertain. Creating a new identity to bypass a difficult search is not an acceptable workaround.

**4. Prerequisite Review**

Where an appointment type requires an administrative prerequisite, staff should confirm the prerequisite through the designated workflow before finalizing. Examples may include an approved referral, authorization step, preparation status or service-specific scheduling condition. Staff should not invent clinical prerequisites.

**5. Appointment Creation Workflow**

The workflow is: identify service; confirm patient record; determine appointment type; check duration and prerequisites; select approved slot; confirm date, time, location and provider/service; save appointment status; communicate approved details; document exceptions. The final review should ensure that the booking reflects what the patient actually requested and what the service can support.

**6. Rescheduling**

Rescheduling should preserve the relevant appointment history and release the original slot through the approved system workflow. The new slot should be checked against appointment type, duration and service requirements. Patients should receive the updated details through an approved channel.

**7. Cancellation**

Cancellations should be recorded with the appropriate status and reason where available. The released capacity should become available through the normal scheduling process. If cancellation affects preparation or another department, the responsible team should be notified through the approved workflow.

**8. Waitlist Management**

Waitlist entries should capture the patient's requested timing, service or specialty and approved communication route. When a suitable slot becomes available, staff should use the approved contact process and update the waitlist status. Repeated unsuccessful contact attempts should be recorded according to local procedure.

**9. No-Shows**

No-shows should be recorded accurately. Follow-up or rebooking should use the approved workflow. Staff should avoid adding unsupported explanations to the record. Repeated no-shows may be reviewed by Patient Services when an operational response is required.

**10. Provider Changes**

When a provider becomes unavailable, scheduling staff should identify affected appointments, locate authorized alternatives and communicate the change. Appointment records should be updated promptly. Staff should not independently interpret clinical urgency; urgent clinical concerns should be routed to the Clinical Team.

**11. Daily Reconciliation**

Daily reconciliation should review cancellations, provider changes, unconfirmed appointments, waitlist opportunities, incomplete bookings and unresolved exceptions. Each unresolved item should have an owner and next action. Reconciliation should occur through the approved operational checklist or system view.

**12. Communication Standards**

Appointment messages should state date, time, location and approved preparation or attendance instructions, together with the route for cancellation or rescheduling. Administrative staff should not add clinical interpretation. Material communication and changes should be recorded when required.

**13. Roles and Escalation**

Scheduling staff manage routine appointments. The Scheduling Lead handles capacity conflicts and unusual scheduling requests. Patient Services coordinates patient-facing exceptions. Clinical staff handle clinical questions and urgency. IT Support handles system issues. Escalations should include the appointment reference, issue, actions taken and required next decision.

Useful measures include booking accuracy, no-show rate, waitlist fill rate, rescheduling volume, provider-change communication completion and unresolved exceptions. Measures should be reviewed with operational context and used to identify process improvements.

**15. Downtime**

During system outages, staff should use the approved downtime workflow. Essential appointment information should be captured securely and reconciled after restoration. The reconciliation should confirm that duplicate bookings or missed updates have not been introduced.

**16. Scenarios and FAQs**

A provider cancellation requires identification of affected patients, approved alternatives and communication. A patient asking for clinical interpretation should be routed to the clinical team. A request for an unsuitable appointment type should be escalated rather than placed into an incorrect slot. A system outage should trigger downtime procedures and post-restoration reconciliation.
